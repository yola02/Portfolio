Safest Countries in the World 2026: Comparing How Safety Is Measured

Four charts built from the same subset of the top 20 safest countries (by Global Peace Index), each presenting the underlying safety data in a different way.

1. Ranking the Safest Countries

This horizontal bar chart ranks the top 20 countries by Travel Safety Index, from Iceland (safest) to Portugal (lowest in this group). Bar length makes it easy to compare every country's score at a glance. Color shows a separate measure — each country's Risk Level rating (Low/Medium/High) — rather than the axis score itself. Portugal is labeled "Low" risk despite having the lowest score here: not an error, but a sign that Risk Level and Travel Safety Index weigh different factors and don't always agree.

2. Zooming In on the Safest of the Safe

This variation switches from bars to dots and narrows the x-axis to 50–100 instead of 0–100. A bar's length has to start at zero to represent magnitude honestly, so truncating a bar chart's axis would be misleading — a dot plot has no such requirement, which is why the mark type changes here too. The narrower range gives the safest countries a bit more visual separation than the full 0–100 scale, though the ranking and pattern are the same as the first chart.

3. Color as Magnitude, Not Category

Here, color encodes the exact same value already shown on the x-axis — Travel Safety Index — using a blue gradient instead of a separate categorical rating. This trades away the extra risk-level information from the first chart, but produces a chart that's internally consistent: the color can never visually disagree with the bar length, since they're both reflecting the same number.

4. Do Two Safety Measures Agree?

This chart drops the ranking format and instead plots two independent safety measures against each other: Global Peace Index on the x-axis and Travel Safety Index on the y-axis. A dashed trend line shows the overall relationship between the two. Most countries sit close to that line, meaning the two measures broadly agree — but Portugal falls well below it, scoring far lower on Travel Safety Index than its Global Peace Index would predict, making it a clear outlier here too.
