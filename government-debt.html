Design Decisions

The original chart is a Sankey chart that links two separate rankings, debt as a share of GDP and debt in dollars, but nothing flows between these measures. Each side shows only its own top 30, so 34 countries have ribbons that fade out with no match on the other side, and 47 overlapping ribbons make it hard to follow any one country. I replaced it with a scatter plot because each country has two numbers, and a scatter plot shows both at once. I kept the same 47 countries as the original so the comparison is fair, and I used a log scale for the dollar axis because debts range from about $1 billion to over $40 trillion. I rejected a slope chart because it has the same problems as the Sankey chart, two bar charts because they cannot show the relationship between the measures, and a map because small countries like Singapore and Bahrain would disappear.

The redesign shows every country with both values and a direct label, so nothing fades out and no ribbons need to be traced. It also shows that only 13 of the 47 countries appear in both of the original rankings. Sudan, Bahrain and the Maldives are heavily indebted compared to their economies but small in dollars, while Germany and India owe a lot of money but less than their whole economy.

Data: IMF World Economic Outlook, April 2026 (IMF data portal). Debt in dollars is debt as a share of GDP multiplied by nominal GDP. Download the dataset | View the Vega-Lite specification
